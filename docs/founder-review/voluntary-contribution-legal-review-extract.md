# Voluntary-contribution wording — legal-review extract

**Review status:** Legal wording review required before production authorisation.

**Purpose:** This is a verbatim extract of wording relevant to founder-led venture contributions, CIC funding separation, and Project Clandestinus reward/prize communication. It is a review aid, not legal advice and not approval for publication.

## Scope of review requested

Ask the solicitor to review:

1. CIC and company-law treatment of any founder contribution, including the CIC asset lock and accounting/governance implications.
2. Fundraising, marketing and consumer-protection implications.
3. Whether a purchase, sale, entry or participation in Project Clandestinus could be understood as funding, supporting or sponsoring the CIC or a particular Tree of Life initiative.
4. The prize-competition and consumer-marketing interface for Project Clandestinus, including any future official rules, reward, verification or activation notices.

## Impact page

**Source:** `src/pages/impact.astro`
**Location:** Opening lead

> Tree of Life combines direct public-benefit initiatives with responsible independent innovation. Successful founder-led ventures may help create resources that can later be contributed to public-benefit work, while each venture remains legally and financially distinct.

**Source:** `src/pages/impact.astro`
**Location:** “Two complementary pathways” → “Independent innovation”

> Founder-led ventures operate independently, with their own ownership, finances, intellectual property, contracts and legal responsibilities. They are not programmes of Visio Nova Mundi CIC, and CIC funding does not automatically finance them.

> No purchase, sale, entry or participation in an independent venture carries a promise that any amount will be transferred to Visio Nova Mundi CIC or to a particular Tree of Life initiative.

> Separately, the founder may from time to time choose to contribute money, resources or other support to Visio Nova Mundi CIC. Any such contribution would be a separate decision, separately recorded and managed in accordance with the CIC’s governance, accounting and public-benefit responsibilities.

> A contribution would not change the ownership, finances, intellectual property, contracts or reward obligations of the independent venture from which the founder's resources may ultimately have arisen.

**Source:** `src/pages/impact.astro`
**Location:** “Clear separation protects the mission”

> Being part of Tree of Life does not transfer an independent venture’s ownership, intellectual property, finances, contracts, publishing activities, prize or reward obligations, or legal responsibilities to Visio Nova Mundi CIC. The same principle protects CIC-led work from being represented as funding or operating a separate venture.

## Official context for the reviewer

- [Office of the Regulator of Community Interest Companies — Community Interest Companies Guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/community-interest-companies-how-to-form-a-cic/community-interest-companies-guidance-chapters): the asset lock applies to CIC assets and assets donated to a CIC become part of the CIC’s assets.
- [HMRC — Community Interest Companies](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/company-taxation-manual/ctm40145): a CIC cannot itself have charitable status and has no CIC-specific tax exemptions or reliefs.
- [Gambling Commission — prize competitions and free draws](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/report/understanding-the-consumer-landscape-in-free-draws-and-prize-competitions/definitions-understanding-the-consumer-landscape-in-free-draws-and-prize): structures that do not meet the applicable exemption requirements may be classified as illegal lotteries; this wording does not itself determine legal compliance.
